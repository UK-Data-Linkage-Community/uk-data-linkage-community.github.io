Our first UK Data Linkage Community workshop took place on the 31st of March. The primary aim of this first session was to identify the current shared and diverging practices of data linkage practitioners around the UK and discuss the objectives and directions of this focused community. Around 30 data linkage practitioners for administrative, governmental, health, and other data contexts attended the day, majoritively in-person but also online. We completed four main sessions, bookended by survey polls and community planning discussions. The workshop was delivered using mostly structured discussions and group activities.

Over the four sessions we saw short talks from key collaborators in the community such as the Ministry of Justice and the ONS, group activities to discuss edge cases, frameworks for presenting to stakeholders, and techniques and delivery of linkage quality metrics. Findings were reported to the larger group, resulting in a curation of priorities and frameworks for each session. We ended the workshop with a discussion of future actions, identifying the need for guidelines for best practices, support materials for non-technical people to complete linkage request forms, pointers to toy datasets, and informative guides for stakeholders to explain terms, align expectations, and advise on linkage caveats.

Here we present the details and outcomes from the first UK DLC workshop, Supporting Data Linkage Practitioners. As the name for the first workshop suggests, the focus was on the current practices and challenges of data linkage practitioners across multiple sectors.
